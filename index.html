Physical model
The Galactic Center is the reference origin. The Sun is placed at R₀ ≈ 8.2 kpc and given a representative ≈230 Myr Galactic orbital period. The planets are translated with the moving Sun, so their absolute trajectories are nested paths rather than circles around a fixed Galactic origin. The Sun’s vertical motion is modeled harmonically in the research mode using Pz ≈ 87.8 Myr, z₀ ≈ +20.8 pc and W ≈ +7.25 km/s.

Important visual distinction
The research mode is the factual harmonic mode. The 4-wave mode forces four vertical oscillations during one 230 Myr teaching orbit by using 57.5 Myr. That is a mathematical teaching construction, not a claim that the Sun is observed to complete exactly four vertical waves per Galactic orbit. The resulting corkscrew appearance is wave-like rather than a perfect constant-pitch helix.

Sun rotation
The purple layer represents the Sun’s axial rotation as a separate local motion. It is not the Sun’s Galactic orbit. The visualization uses the Sun’s differential-rotation period approximately 25 days at the equator and 36 days near the poles, with the 7.25° solar-spin-axis tilt represented schematically.

Absolute Solar-System corkscrew
In the corkscrew view, the Galactic Center remains the fixed reference while the Sun follows its Galactic orbital motion with vertical oscillation. Every planet is positioned as Sun(t) plus its heliocentric Keplerian vector, so the planets are physically transported with the moving Sun. Their small heliocentric orbits are therefore nested inside the large-scale wave-like carrier path rather than drawn around a stationary Sun.

Planetary realism
The eight planets use nominal semi-major axes, eccentricities, inclinations and sidereal periods with Kepler-equation propagation. Their absolute position is the sum of the moving Sun’s Galactic position and the heliocentric planet position. Planet sizes and local orbit dimensions are deliberately enlarged for visibility. This is not a real-time ephemeris or N-body integration.

Galaxy structure
The 25,000 stellar nodes are deterministic visualization samples. The bar/boxy-peanut core, spiral density structure, halo and outer warp are schematic. Warp precession is shown as an outer-disc structural diagnostic and must not be confused with the Sun’s local vertical oscillation.
